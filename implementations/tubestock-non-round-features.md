# Tube stock: cutting shapes that are not round holes

Design for letting tube stock AutoCAM actually *mill* slots, keyways and
other non-circular wall features as real functional geometry. Nothing here
is implemented yet - this is still a design doc, not code.

**Do not confuse this with the cutoff line (merged, #260/#256/#257/#258/
#259).** The cutoff is one specific, fixed obround shape this app draws and
mills itself from an operator-entered length - it never reads a non-round
shape out of a STEP file. What this doc is about is the opposite direction:
a non-round shape that already exists in the CAD model and needs to be cut
as the part's own real geometry, not generated by this app.

## What happens today

`extractTubeFeaturesFromMeshes` used to refuse a non-round wall feature
outright and fail the whole tube over it - the exact error the `test` job in
the jobs list failed with:

> A feature on this tube isn't round (radius varies 17% around its boundary)
> - likely a slot, keyway, or other non-circular cutout that indexed
> round-hole drilling can't represent.

**That specific failure is fixed** (merged into #260's branch): a non-round
loop is skipped and reported (`wall.skippedFeatures`) instead of throwing,
so the tube's other real round holes still get drilled. Checked against the
actual STEP file behind the `test` job: the 2 loops that were failing it
turned out to be obround witness marks the CAD modeler drew for their own
reference, not holes meant to be cut - skipping them was the correct
outcome, not a workaround for a gap this doc still needs to fill.

That fix does not mean this doc is done. It means an operator now finds out
a feature was skipped instead of the job dying, which is strictly better,
but a non-round feature that genuinely IS meant to be milled - a real slot
or keyway, not a witness mark - still isn't cut. Nothing here mills it yet.

## The useful part: the geometry is already there

Milling a non-round feature is not a gap in extraction. `stepProfile.js`
around line 875 already does all the work:

1. It finds every closed boundary loop on a wall.
2. It sorts them by area and treats the largest as the wall's own outline.
3. Every remaining loop is a feature.
4. For each feature it computes a centroid, a mean radius, and the maximum
   deviation from that radius. If the deviation exceeds
   `MAX_HOLE_RADIUS_VARIATION` (0.15), it is now skipped into
   `wall.skippedFeatures` rather than thrown (see above) - but still
   skipped, not cut.

So a slot's outline is **already extracted as a polygon** and then discarded
- just no longer at the cost of the rest of the tube. Supporting non-round
features doesn't need new STEP work. It needs that polygon carried through
into `holes` (or somewhere a generator can reach it) instead of being routed
to `skippedFeatures`, and a contour toolpath instead of a drill cycle.

## What has to change

**1. Classify into three, not two.** The skip-and-report branch already
exists (see above). What's missing is a real third outcome alongside "round
hole" and "skipped, not cut": a profile, described by its polygon in the
same wall coordinates holes already use (`position` along the tube,
`lateralOffset` across the face) and carried somewhere a generator can
reach it - a new `profiles` array on the wall sits better than overloading
`skippedFeatures`, which by name now means "seen and deliberately not cut."
The threshold still tells round from not-round; it's what happens to "not
round" that needs a real choice instead of always meaning "skip."

**2. Emit a contour rather than a plunge.** `generateTubestockGcode` walks
holes and emits a plunge per hole. A profile needs a contoured pass at
depth, which is what `routing.js` already does for sheets. Two pieces there
are directly reusable and should be reused rather than reimplemented:

- `offsetPolygon(points, distance)` for cutter compensation, so the cut
  lands on the line rather than centred on it. **Now precedented, not just
  proposed**: the cutoff feature (#260) already does exactly this -
  `tubestock.js`'s `buildCutoffFeature` offsets a drawn obround inward by
  the tool radius and mills it in one lap. Whatever this builds should
  reuse that same call, not a second one.
- `cornerFeedScale(prev, cur, next)` for slowing into corners. Not used by
  the cutoff (its ends are already curved, and it runs at drilling-speed
  feed throughout), but still the right tool for a profile with real sharp
  corners.

What is *not* fully settled is the depth model, though the cutoff narrows
it. A sheet is cut in step-downs to a target depth with tabs holding the
part in place. The cutoff instead goes through its wall in a single pass at
`holeDepth` - no step-down, one lap - because that is what a THROUGH cut on
tube-wall-thin material actually needs, and it is the pattern to reuse if
question 1 below answers "through the wall." It says nothing about a
POCKET, which is a different depth entirely and has no precedent here yet.
There is also still nothing to hold a slug in on a through cut - it falls
into the tube - so that part of the question stands (see open questions).

**3. Simulate it.** The simulator draws drilled holes into wall boxes. A
profile is a swept cut, closer to what the routing heightmap already does.
Per-face simulation lands first, so this can build on it.

## What this does not cover

Anything requiring the cutter to reach a wall it cannot see from above. The
operator turns the tube by hand and each face is cut in its own setup, so a
feature that spans a corner between two faces is out of scope. It should
keep the refusal it has now, with a message that says why rather than
"isn't round".

## Open questions

1. **Through the wall, or a pocket?** A through slot and a pocket floored
   partway into the wall are different toolpaths, and only one of them can
   reuse the wall-thickness depth from #255. Which is actually wanted?
2. **What happens to the slug?** Cutting a closed profile through a wall
   frees the material inside it, and inside a tube it drops into the cavity
   rather than staying put. Is that acceptable, or does it need tabs the way
   sheet parts do?
3. **Cutter fit.** A slot narrower than the end mill cannot be cut at all,
   and one only slightly wider leaves no room to compensate. Refuse it, or
   cut what fits and report the difference?
4. **Order.** Should profiles be cut before or after the holes on the same
   face? Cutting a large opening first can leave the wall flexing under a
   later drill.
